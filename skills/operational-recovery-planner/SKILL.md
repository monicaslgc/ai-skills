# Operational Recovery Planner

## Purpose
Turn an agreed diagnosis into a realistic recovery plan with clear accountability and decision points.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the recovery objective, scope, timeframe, decision authority and any immediate containment requirements.
2. Summarise verified baseline performance, data limitations, risks and the hypotheses that still need testing.
3. Separate stabilisation from diagnosis, corrective action and sustained recovery; avoid launching every intervention at once.
4. Define a small set of outcome and counter-metrics, with metric definitions, baselines and target values supplied or approved by the owner.
5. Build phases with actions, dependencies, accountable roles to be confirmed, milestones, evidence of completion and review dates agreed by the user.
6. Define decision gates: continue, adapt, pause or escalate, including what evidence would trigger each choice.
7. Add sustainability checks so a short-term rebound is not mistaken for durable recovery.
8. Summarise unresolved decisions, resource needs and the approval required before execution.

## Guardrails
Do not promise recovery or invent milestones, owners, budgets or targets. Avoid punitive assumptions about people; test process, market, capacity, training, tools and data factors. Do not recommend closure, employment action or other consequential decisions as automatic outputs.

## Output contract
- Recovery objective and baseline
- Stabilisation and diagnosis actions
- Phased action plan with owners to confirm
- Milestones, metrics and decision gates
- Risks, dependencies and approvals
- Sustainability review

## Acceptance checks
- Actions link to evidence or explicitly stated hypotheses.
- Each phase has a measurable verification point.
- Targets and dates are provided or clearly awaiting approval.
- No intervention is presented as guaranteed to work.

## Example prompt
"Draft a phased operational recovery plan from this evidence. Separate stabilisation, diagnosis and corrective action; define review gates and metrics without inventing targets, owners or dates."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, grounds material claims in supplied evidence, handles at least one edge case, labels uncertainty and proposes a concrete validation or follow-up step. Fail if it invents data or bypasses an approval boundary.
