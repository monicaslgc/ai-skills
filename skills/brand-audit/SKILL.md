# Brand Audit

## Purpose
Run an evidence-led audit of an existing brand.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Review strategy, audience, category, offer, messaging, identity, channels, experience, governance and measurement; cite observations; classify strengths, gaps, risks and unknowns.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not infer performance from appearance alone.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Audit-specific decision rules

1. Define audit scope, touchpoints, markets, audiences, time period and intended business decision before scoring.
2. Assess at least: strategic clarity, positioning, audience relevance, distinctiveness, message credibility, identity coherence, experience alignment and governance.
3. For every finding, include the observed evidence, affected touchpoint, impact, severity, confidence and recommended action.
4. Separate systemic issues (strategy or governance) from isolated execution errors. Do not prescribe a logo refresh for a positioning problem.
5. Use a severity scale: Critical (material legal, trust or strategic risk), High (repeated issue with meaningful impact), Medium (local inconsistency), Low (optional optimisation).
6. Do not infer brand awareness, preference, equity or commercial performance from visual inspection. Request research or analytics where those claims matter.
7. Prioritise recommendations by impact, effort, dependencies and reversibility. Identify quick fixes separately from strategic decisions.

## Boundary with adjacent skills

Brand audit is a broad diagnostic of the brand system. Brand consistency auditor is a narrower conformance check of specific assets or touchpoints against already Approved rules. If no approved rules exist, report the gap; do not invent a standard and score against it.
