# Brand Discovery

## Purpose
Collect the evidence and context needed before proposing a brand direction.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Collect business model, offer, audience, geography, stage, goals, constraints, existing assets and stakeholder views; identify missing evidence; build a discovery summary.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not invent customer insight or treat stakeholder opinion as market evidence.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Discovery-specific decision rules

1. Start with the decision the project must make, not with a generic brand questionnaire.
2. Build an evidence map across business, offer, audience, category, customer experience and existing brand assets. For each insight, record source, date, sample/context and confidence.
3. Distinguish what the organisation says it is from what customers experience and what the market signals. Treat these as separate evidence streams.
4. Identify the highest-impact unknowns. Recommend interviews, analytics review, customer research or desk research only when the method can resolve a stated uncertainty.
5. Do not write positioning, taglines or visual concepts during discovery unless explicitly requested; capture inputs and implications instead.
6. End with a discovery brief containing: decision to make, evidence map, audience hypotheses, category context, constraints, contradictions, priority research gaps and recommended next stage.
7. Escalate conflicting stakeholder claims rather than averaging them into false consensus.

## Boundary with adjacent skills

Discovery gathers and organises evidence. The workshop facilitator runs a live collaborative session and records what participants decide. Brand audit evaluates an existing brand against evidence and defined dimensions. Do not substitute one activity for another.
