# Capacity planning forecast

Show how much work is coming, how much capacity you have, and what changes if the assumptions are wrong.

## Inputs to collect

Ask for what is missing, or mark it as unknown:
- Historical volumes by period and type of work.
- Time needed per item (handling time), including variation.
- Available hours per person after leave, training and meetings (shrinkage).
- Service targets, such as response times.

## Workflow

1. Clean the history: remove one-off spikes you can explain, and note seasonality and trends.
2. Forecast volume per period with a simple method (recent average with seasonal adjustment) and state its limits.
3. Convert volume into required hours using handling time.
4. Calculate productive hours per person after shrinkage: leave, breaks, training, meetings, idle time.
5. Compute the staff required per period and compare it with the staff available. Show shortfalls and surpluses.
6. Build three scenarios (expected, high, low) and a what-if for changes such as a new product or a handling-time improvement.
7. Recommend actions: hiring, overtime, cross-training, deferral of non-urgent work, automation. State the risk of each.
8. List assumptions and how to refresh the forecast.

## Output format

| Period | Forecast volume | Handling time | Required hours | Productive hours per person | Required staff | Available staff | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Scenarios, assumptions, recommendations.

## Safety

- Label forecasts as estimates and show the assumptions.
- Do not use the output to evaluate individual people.
- Use anonymised or synthetic data in shared examples.
