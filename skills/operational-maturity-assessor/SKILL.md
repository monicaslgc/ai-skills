# Operational Maturity Assessor

## Purpose
Help teams understand how consistently operational practices are defined, executed, measured and improved.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the scope, purpose, participants and intended use of the assessment.
2. Agree assessment dimensions relevant to the operation, such as process clarity, ownership, measurement, exception handling, knowledge retention and improvement.
3. Define observable maturity levels before scoring; use a supplied framework or propose draft criteria for human approval.
4. Collect evidence for each dimension, distinguishing documented policy from demonstrated practice.
5. Assign a level only where evidence supports it; otherwise mark unknown or provisionally assessed.
6. Summarise strengths, gaps, evidence confidence and operational consequences without treating the score as a universal benchmark.
7. Propose a small set of staged improvement actions with owners and review dates to be confirmed.

## Guardrails
Do not claim certification or compliance from a maturity assessment. Do not invent a recognised framework or present draft levels as industry standards. Avoid scoring people; assess practices and systems. Scores are provisional unless the assessment method and evidence have been reviewed.

## Output contract
- Scope and assessment method
- Maturity criteria and evidence by dimension
- Provisional profile with confidence
- Strengths, gaps and limitations
- Prioritised improvement roadmap

## Acceptance checks
- Criteria are defined and visible before scoring.
- Every assigned level cites observable evidence.
- Unknowns remain unknown rather than being guessed.
- No certification or universal benchmark is implied.

## Example prompt
"Assess operational maturity from this evidence. Define or apply explicit criteria, cite observable practices, mark unknowns and produce a proportionate improvement roadmap without claiming certification."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, grounds material claims in supplied evidence, handles edge cases, labels uncertainty and proposes concrete validation. Fail if it invents facts or bypasses approval boundaries.
