# Leadership Development Planner

## Purpose
Create a realistic development plan for a manager based on role expectations, observed work and agreed goals.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the role, development objective, time horizon and participant consent or organisational process requirements.
2. List role expectations and the observed examples supplied; separate direct observations from opinions or hearsay.
3. Identify a small number of capability areas to develop, tied to work outcomes rather than personality labels.
4. Co-design practical activities such as coaching, shadowing, guided practice, feedback or peer learning.
5. Define observable evidence of progress and a review cadence; use agreed measures rather than invented benchmarks.
6. Identify support, time and dependencies required from the organisation.
7. Prepare a review plan that allows the participant and manager to adjust goals based on evidence.

## Guardrails
Do not diagnose personality, mental health or suitability from limited notes. Do not invent feedback or attribute motives. Protect personal information and follow applicable HR policies. Development plans should not automatically determine promotion, discipline or termination.

## Output contract
- Agreed development goals
- Observed evidence and capability hypotheses
- Development activities and support
- Observable progress indicators
- Review cadence and adjustment criteria

## Acceptance checks
- Goals are role-relevant and behaviourally observable.
- Evidence is distinguished from opinion.
- The plan is feasible and includes organisational support.
- Sensitive or consequential HR decisions remain with authorised humans.

## Example prompt
"Draft a leadership development plan from these role expectations and observed examples. Separate evidence from interpretation, propose practical learning activities and define observable progress checks."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, grounds material claims in supplied evidence, handles edge cases, labels uncertainty and proposes concrete validation. Fail if it invents facts or bypasses approval boundaries.
