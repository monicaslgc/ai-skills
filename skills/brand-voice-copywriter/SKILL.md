# Brand Voice Copywriter

## Purpose
Create a practical verbal identity and on-brand copy.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Define voice principles, tone shifts, vocabulary, syntax, examples and anti-examples; produce sample copy for key touchpoints; explain deviations.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not invent product claims, guarantees, testimonials or credentials.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Verbal-identity-specific decision rules

1. Read the approved positioning and messaging framework before defining voice. If they are missing, label the work as a provisional exploration.
2. Define 3–5 observable voice principles. For each, describe what it means, what it does not mean, linguistic signals and a before/after example.
3. Define tone shifts by context (for example, support, product UI, sales, crisis communication) without changing the underlying personality.
4. Provide vocabulary guidance, sentence patterns, punctuation preferences, inclusive-language guidance and prohibited patterns.
5. Demonstrate the voice on representative touchpoints and include anti-examples. Avoid relying on abstract adjectives such as "bold" or "friendly" without behavioural evidence.
6. Preserve factual meaning and legal disclaimers when rewriting. Never add unsupported product benefits, guarantees, testimonials or credentials.
7. Deliver a usable voice guide plus copy examples, explaining any deliberate deviations by channel.

## Boundary with adjacent skills

Messaging framework determines what the brand needs to communicate and to whom. Voice copywriter determines how that message is expressed. If the source message is unclear or unsupported, flag it rather than polishing it into a confident claim.
