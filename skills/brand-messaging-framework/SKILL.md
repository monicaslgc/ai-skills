# Brand Messaging Framework

## Purpose
Build a message hierarchy for audiences and channels.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Create core narrative, value proposition, audience-specific messages, proof points, objections, elevator pitch and channel adaptations; link claims to evidence.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Keep proof points separate from aspirations and unverified claims.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Messaging-specific decision rules

1. Anchor the framework in an Approved positioning and a clearly defined priority audience. If either is missing, mark the messaging as provisional.
2. Build a hierarchy: core promise, supporting pillars, proof points, audience-specific messages, objections and calls to action.
3. For every factual or comparative claim, record the evidence owner and substantiation. Route risky, regulated or quantified claims to the claim validator.
4. Separate the stable message (what must remain consistent) from channel adaptation (what may change by format, audience or funnel stage).
5. Avoid generic benefit language. Explain the audience problem, relevant outcome, differentiation and why the organisation can credibly deliver it.
6. Test the framework against real audience questions and objections; do not claim it is validated without research.
7. Deliver a message matrix with audience, need, message, proof, objection, CTA, channel adaptation and confidence.

## Boundary with adjacent skills

This skill defines message architecture and content hierarchy. Brand voice copywriter defines how the brand sounds and applies that voice to actual copy. Do not let tone-of-voice choices replace strategic message decisions.
