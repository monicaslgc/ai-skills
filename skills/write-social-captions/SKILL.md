# Write social captions

For a creator who posts gaming content (fashion, virtual cosplay, outfit and ability showcases, gacha pulls, short jokes, cozy games). Never assume the channel covers a single game.

## Voice (always)

- Ask which language public text should be in; default to English, even if the chat is in another language.
- Sound like a real player: direct, warm, playful, sometimes dry or self-deprecating. A short clip gets a short joke.
- No em dashes in public copy.
- Write rarity as emoji stars (5⭐, 4⭐) unless the creator's wording says otherwise. Their exact wording and authentic logos win.
- Avoid: "obsessed", "queen", "ethereal", "magical journey", "dive into", generic hype and forced questions. Do not end every cosplay caption with the same question.
- Never add claims about AI use or non-use. Never claim work is handmade or unedited unless told.
- Give one recommended finished set. Give alternatives only when asked or when choosing the hook is the task. No lecture around usable copy.
- For a caption-only request, give paste-ready text and no report.

## Before writing

Know the game, event, mood, character or outfit, what actually happens, the clip structure, the platforms and the language. Infer from footage and context; do not run a questionnaire for a simple caption. If the creator says the video is fully edited, respect its joke and pacing and do not suggest re-edits.

Verify time-sensitive or niche details (spelling, game, rarity, version, release status, collaboration) from official material or direct footage. Keep the creator's shorthand while checking what it means. If two characters genuinely fit, ask one specific question. Never "correct" a name to the wrong franchise. Do not mix games: gameplay of one game gets no tags for another, while a dress-up character styled as a character from a second game involves both.

On-video text, platform caption and thumbnail text are three different outputs. Do not mix them.

## On-video text

Give one recommended line, plus two or three alternatives with different angles only when useful. If a one-liner is requested, give one. It must be readable within the shot's hold time at phone size (6 to 12 words is a starting point). Do not explain the whole clip on screen. Pick by function:
- Reveal: name the inspiration or promise the transformation.
- Ability showcase: make the useful or surprising behaviour obvious.
- Failure joke: set up the expectation without spoiling the reveal.
- Appreciation: say what the creator loves, backed by what is shown.
- Before an update: anticipation, never implying it is already live.
- Piece-by-piece: make clear viewers will see the individual items.

Keep factual overlays separate from jokes. A personal "still my favourite ability" is fine; "the best ability in the game" is a claim.

## Platform output

| Platform | Deliver | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | Caption + hashtags | Direct personal voice; add a second joke beat or useful context, do not narrate every frame |
| Instagram Reels | Caption + hashtags (cover line only if asked) | Name the look or inspiration clearly, tidy spacing, a bit more styling detail |
| YouTube Shorts | Title, description, hashtags | Searchable subject plus real curiosity; the first description sentence says what is shown |
| YouTube long-form | 3 title options + recommendation, description; chapters only after the final edit | Promise the real build, test or story; separate pulling from testing |

"All platforms" means TikTok, Reels and Shorts. A landscape YouTube upload gets long-form packaging, not Shorts packaging. Never post identical copy with only the platform label changed.

YouTube: put the recognisable game, character or outfit naturally in the title; state the action (styling, pulling, testing, build); keep titles around 40 to 70 characters; make the description add two or three real details instead of repeating the title; for cosplay name both the base game and the inspiration without claiming an official crossover; for pulls never claim a win, attempt count or spend that was not supplied. Do not "fix" weak views by lengthening the description.

## Hashtags

Three to five by default; never exceed an explicit maximum. Roles: the actual game; the exact character or outfit if useful; the real format (cosplay, outfit showcase); a relevant event or crossover; optionally an established brand tag. A smaller accurate set beats filling a quota. No unrelated franchises, misspellings or #viral/#fyp filler. Never invent tag volumes or promise reach.

## Content-type rules

- **Inspired looks:** both games belong in context when a dress-up character is styled as another game's character. Say "inspired by" or "reimagined", never imply an official crossover. Do not apologise for wardrobe limits. See `style-inspired-cosplay-looks`.
- **Showcases:** classify first (piece-by-piece, whole look, ability test, hidden interaction, dye comparison). Name outfits or items only when verified, never from appearance. Say only what the recording proves ("I used this to cross this obstacle", not "this skips all puzzles"). No "exclusive" or "new" unless verified.
- **Pulls, failures, memes:** keep a ledger: intended target, expectation, actual outcome, reveal time, reaction. Do not assume the outcome from the target. Keep the ironic setup and let the reveal be the joke. Use the creator's stated spending framing, never an invented amount. For audio IDs use what is audible or an original post, give uncertain candidates as uncertain, never invent a track title, and check licensing before naming audio in exports. Exaggerated doodles ("a million 5⭐") must not become a factual count.
- **Voiceover explainers and info images:** build a fact table first (claim, amount, eligibility, time window, free or paid, source, verified date). Separate confirmed, estimated, recurring and conditional rewards; avoid double counting; show the arithmetic in working notes; write a natural voiceover and check its spoken length; one idea per image; no frame-accurate timestamps before an edit exists.
- **Campaigns and challenges:** the supplied prompt list is the authority. Transcribe every prompt and date and compare back. Do not call a community challenge official without evidence. Say the creator is taking part; do not promise a daily schedule unless they committed.
- **Time context:** do not carry "in a few hours" or a maintenance joke into a later posting date.

## Corrections the creator has given

If the creator keeps a corrections file, read it before writing; it wins over this file. Typical rules worth confirming:
- Never call a look a "collab" unless the notes say so; do not mention it at all unless the post is about one.
- Never guess outfit or piece names from the screen; use names only from the creator's notes, the album title or official sources.
- Day-N copy says "first" only when N = 1; no "full breakdown in the video" in a Short.
- Never reuse a caption line that already went out.
- Describe only what is clearly visible; if unsure, describe colour and mood, not objects.
- Write about the look, the prompt and the game, never about making the video.
- A post is a repeat only if it is the exact same video. Same level, game, objects or topic is not a repeat.

## Delivery

On-video text only when requested or clearly part of the brief. Then label TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, with the YouTube title and description separate, each caption kept with its tags. No source citations inside the caption unless the post needs attribution. Put short factual source notes after the copy. Use plain delimited text, no app-specific markup.

## Safety

- Do not post, schedule or send anything.
- Do not state spending, results or release facts that were not supplied or verified.
