# Brand Positioning

## Purpose
Develop and compare positioning options that make an offering's value meaningful to a defined audience relative to the alternatives they actually consider.

## Knowledge dependencies
- **Mode:** Hybrid.
- **Required knowledge:** [Brand Fundamentals](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/BRAND_FUNDAMENTALS.md), [Strategy and Positioning](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/STRATEGY_AND_POSITIONING.md).
- **Supporting knowledge:** [Research and Evidence](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/RESEARCH_AND_EVIDENCE.md) when available and relevant.
- Read the required knowledge before developing options. Apply the decision criteria; do not copy the knowledge module into the deliverable.
- If knowledge files are inaccessible, state that limitation, apply only the principles available in this skill and avoid claiming the full knowledge base was consulted.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the positioning decision required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Discovery evidence, current alternatives, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
1. Define the audience and decision/use situation.
2. Identify what that audience would do if the offering did not exist, including direct competitors, substitutes, manual workarounds, status quo and doing nothing.
3. Identify capabilities that are genuinely different relative to those alternatives.
4. Translate capabilities into functional consequences and audience value.
5. Identify best-fit segments who care most about that value; describe poor-fit segments too.
6. Choose candidate category frames that make the value understandable and credible.
7. Generate materially different positioning territories, not cosmetic rewrites.
8. Evaluate relevance, distinctiveness, credibility, focus, durability, feasibility, comprehension and decision utility.
9. State evidence gaps, trade-offs and proof requirements; request approval before locking positioning.

## Output
1. **Positioning options:** concise statement plus plain-language explanation for each territory.
2. **Audience and situation:** who values the offer, when and why.
3. **Competitive alternatives:** evidenced alternatives, including status quo and workarounds.
4. **Differentiated value:** capability → consequence → customer value.
5. **Proof:** direct evidence, proxy evidence, assertions and hypotheses clearly distinguished.
6. **Comparison:** criteria, rationale, trade-offs and unresolved questions.
7. **Implications:** product, service, operations, sales and communications.
8. **Decision needed:** owner and approval required.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve supplied facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not claim uniqueness without competitor and category evidence.
- Do not infer customer preference from stakeholder opinion.
- Do not confuse positioning with messaging, tagline or visual identity.
- Do not use numeric scores as objective truth; explain criteria and weighting.
- Follow shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Category is a frame, not a differentiator by itself. Choose it after understanding alternatives, capabilities, value and best-fit customers.
- A capability counts as differentiated only relative to an alternative and when it matters to the audience.
- A reason to believe must support the specific promise; proxies and testimonials do not automatically prove typical outcomes.
- Generate options with genuinely different strategic implications.
- Test each difference against competitor/alternative evidence and actual organisational capability.
- Identify who the positioning excludes and what trade-offs it creates.
- Mark unsupported differentiation as a hypothesis; require owner approval before locking positioning.
