# Audience Research

## Purpose
Develop audience segments and research questions without fabricating personas.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Review interviews, analytics, surveys and support evidence; identify behavioural needs, jobs, barriers and contexts; distinguish observed patterns from hypotheses; propose validation research.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Never invent demographic or psychographic facts. Label provisional segments clearly.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Define the business decision and population before segmenting; do not start from demographic stereotypes.
- Segment by evidenced needs, behaviours, context, constraints or jobs-to-be-done. Explain why each segment is actionable and distinct.
- For each segment, record evidence source, sample size/context, recency, confidence and known bias.
- Keep observed behaviour separate from inferred motivation. Use provisional labels until validated.
- Recommend the next research method based on the uncertainty: interviews for motivations, analytics for observed behaviour, surveys for prevalence, usability tests for task friction.
- Do not imply statistical representativeness from convenience samples.
- Deliver segment cards, evidence matrix, unresolved hypotheses and a validation plan.
