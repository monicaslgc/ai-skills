# Cv tailoring from master

Use one complete source document and cut it down to the best honest match for each application.

## Inputs to collect

Ask for what is missing, or mark it as unknown:
- The master CV, which is the only source of facts.
- The job posting.
- Target length and any format rules.
- Things to keep out (confidential names, personal data).

## Workflow

1. Read the posting and list its must-haves, nice-to-haves, keywords and seniority signals.
2. Map each must-have to evidence in the master CV. If there is no evidence, record it as a gap. Do not invent it.
3. Choose the positioning headline and summary variant that best fits the role.
4. Select the highlights, competencies and bullets that prove the must-haves. Cut what is irrelevant. Lead with the strongest evidence.
5. Use the posting's words where they are true for the candidate, so the CV passes keyword screening without exaggeration.
6. Keep facts consistent with the master and with other public profiles: titles, dates, numbers.
7. Check the format: plain structure for ATS, one or two pages, consistent tenses, no filler words, and no personal data the candidate wants omitted.
8. Return the tailored CV, a short list of what was cut and why, and the gaps the candidate may address in a cover letter or interview.

## Output format

```
Posting analysis: must-haves, nice-to-haves, keywords
Evidence map: requirement -> CV evidence | gap
Tailored CV (positioning, summary, highlights, competencies, experience bullets, skills, certifications)
Changes made and what was cut
Gaps and how to address them
```

## Safety

- Never add skills, titles, employers, dates or numbers that are not in the master CV.
- Respect confidentiality: generalise names the candidate cannot disclose.
- Do not include date of birth, ID numbers, home address or photo unless the candidate asks for it.
