# Project charter writer

Turn a vague project idea into a short charter that sponsors can approve and teams can work from.

## Inputs to collect

Ask for what is missing, or mark it as unknown:
- The problem or opportunity, in the requester's words.
- Who sponsors, decides and is affected.
- Known deadlines, budget range and constraints.
- What has already been decided or is out of scope.

## Workflow

1. Write a one-sentence problem statement, then the purpose in one sentence.
2. Set 3-5 objectives. Each needs a measure, a baseline if known, and a target date.
3. List what is in scope and, just as important, what is explicitly out of scope.
4. Name stakeholders with their role and decision rights (who decides, who is consulted, who is informed).
5. State constraints, assumptions and dependencies. Mark each assumption as confirmed or unconfirmed.
6. List 4-8 high-level milestones with target dates or date ranges.
7. List the top three risks with a first response each.
8. Add the approval block and list open questions that must be answered before kickoff.

## Output format

```
# Project charter: <name>
Problem / Purpose
Objectives (measure, baseline, target, date)
Scope: in / out
Stakeholders and decision rights
Constraints, assumptions, dependencies
Milestones
Top risks
Budget range
Approvals (name, role, date)
Open questions
```
Keep it to one page. Use plain language.

## Safety

- Do not invent budgets, dates or approvals. Mark unknowns as open questions.
- Do not present an unapproved charter as signed off.
