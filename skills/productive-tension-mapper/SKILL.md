# Productive Tension Mapper

## Purpose
Make disagreement useful. Reveal what different ideas are optimising for, what cannot currently coexist and what new options might emerge from the tension.

## Procedure
1. Extract the competing propositions in neutral language.
2. Identify the underlying value, need, assumption or constraint behind each.
3. Classify the tension where useful:
   - value vs value;
   - short-term vs long-term;
   - simplicity vs richness;
   - control vs freedom;
   - consistency vs expression;
   - scale vs intimacy;
   - certainty vs experimentation;
   - user need vs organisational constraint.
4. Distinguish a genuine incompatibility from a false binary or missing information.
5. Explore at least one response:
   - sequence the competing needs;
   - serve different contexts or segments;
   - create a boundary or adjustable choice;
   - combine the underlying values through a new mechanism;
   - run a small experiment to learn which matters more;
   - preserve the tension as an explicit design principle.
6. State what remains unresolved.

## Rules
- Do not flatten disagreement into "both are important".
- Do not assume compromise is always best.
- Do not invent stakeholders, motives or evidence.
- Keep each side's strongest argument intact.
- Avoid treating a constraint as negotiable unless the user says it is.
- Do not turn exploration into a decision or score unless requested.

## Output format
- **Tension:** A vs B
- **What each side protects**
- **False binary or real conflict?**
- **Ways through:** two or three distinct moves
- **Open question / experiment**

## Acceptance checks
- Both sides are represented fairly.
- Underlying values or assumptions are made explicit.
- At least one non-obvious path is explored when possible.
- Unresolved trade-offs remain visible.
- No decision is attributed to the user without approval.
