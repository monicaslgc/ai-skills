# Market Category Analysis

## Purpose
Map a market category and its communication conventions.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Define category boundaries, substitutes, geography and segments; gather dated sources; compare offers, claims, language and visual codes; identify conventions and opportunities.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- State search limits and avoid unsupported market-size estimates.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Define the category by customer problem and alternatives, not only by industry labels used by suppliers.
- Specify geography, segment, time period, inclusion criteria and source hierarchy.
- Map direct offers, substitutes, do-it-yourself options and non-consumption where relevant.
- Analyse category language, proof conventions, price/packaging signals and visual codes from observable public evidence.
- Quantify market size only when a defensible source and methodology exist; state currency, year, scope and limitations.
- Distinguish category-wide patterns from a small sample of visible brands.
- Deliver category map, evidence register, convention/whitespace analysis and implications for positioning.
