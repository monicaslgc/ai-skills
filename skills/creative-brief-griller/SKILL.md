# Creative Brief Griller

## Purpose
Turn a vague or solution-loaded request into a useful creative challenge while preserving ambiguity that is intentional and productive.

## When to use
Use when the brief is incomplete, contradictory, framed as a predetermined solution, or likely to send ideation in the wrong direction. Skip it when the user has already supplied enough context.

## Procedure
1. **Reflect the brief.** Summarise what the user appears to be trying to achieve in plain language.
2. **Separate the elements.** Identify desired outcome, people or systems affected, current situation, friction, context, constraints, non-goals, supplied evidence, assumptions and unknowns.
3. **Find the tension.** Ask what is difficult, unsatisfactory, surprising or unresolved. Distinguish the stated request from the underlying need.
4. **Challenge the frame.** Offer alternative problem statements when the original wording embeds an untested solution or excludes useful possibilities.
5. **Ask focused questions.** Ask one high-impact question at a time. Prefer questions whose answers would materially change the exploration. Do not ask for information that is not needed yet.
6. **Agree a working brief.** Summarise the challenge, outcome, constraints, non-goals and open assumptions. Invite correction without requiring formal sign-off for low-stakes exploration.

## Question prompts
Choose only what is relevant:
- What would be different if this worked?
- Who experiences the problem, and in what situation?
- What happens today, and what is frustrating about it?
- Is this a fixed constraint or an assumption we could challenge?
- What must remain true?
- What have you already tried or ruled out?
- Are we solving the underlying problem or improving a proposed solution?
- What would make an answer genuinely useful?

## Guardrails
- Do not turn every brief into a long discovery interview.
- Do not lead the user toward a feature, product or familiar solution.
- Do not treat audience, budget, technology or deadlines as known unless supplied.
- Do not invent research or claim that a problem is validated.
- Do not remove useful uncertainty too early; mark it as an exploration area.
- Do not confuse a constraint with a preference.
- Respect requests to brainstorm immediately; state important assumptions and proceed.

## Output contract
Provide a concise **Working Brief**: challenge; desired outcome; relevant people and context; known constraints and non-goals; evidence supplied; assumptions and unknowns; suggested creative question (optional).

## Acceptance checks
- The brief distinguishes the requested solution from the underlying challenge.
- Only material gaps are questioned.
- Assumptions and unknowns are explicit.
- No unsupported facts are added.
- The resulting brief guides ideation without prescribing the answer.

## Example prompt
"Help me clarify this brief: we need a more engaging way for people to learn a complicated process. Challenge the assumptions before suggesting solutions."
