# Brand Naming Validation

## Purpose
Validate a shortlist before a naming decision.

## Required inputs
- User objective and required decision or deliverable.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Read `../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/NAMING_METHOD_PLAYBOOK.md`. Validate names as spoken, heard, written, remembered and used in context: pronunciation at first sight, spelling after hearing once, delayed recall, expected associations, conversation use, brand-architecture fit and competitive distinctiveness. Check priority languages with native speakers where possible. Consume the evidence from `brand-name-availability` rather than repeating its domain/company/trademark work by default. Check social handles or refresh time-sensitive evidence only when required; log platform/jurisdiction, exact query, date and result.

## Output
1. Outcome: requested artefact or recommendation.
2. Evidence: source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. Assumptions and unknowns, separate from verified facts.
4. Decisions needed, owner and approval required.
5. Smallest useful next step.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates.
- Protect confidential and personal information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Search results are preliminary screening, not legal clearance or proof of availability.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Confirm exact candidate spelling, intended goods/services, target jurisdictions, languages and launch timeframe.
- Apply the playbook's first-sight, dictation, delayed-recall, association, conversation, architecture and competitive-shelf tests where the stage and access to participants allow.
- Screen pronunciation, hearability, spelling variants, meanings, unintended associations, cross-language risks and confusion risk with native-language review where relevant.
- Record test method, participant context, actual observations and limitations; never present informal feedback as statistically representative research.
- Treat the `brand-name-availability` report as the source for preliminary domain, company-register and trademark screening. Check that mandatory constraints were covered and evidence is current; send gaps back to that skill rather than silently duplicating research.
- Check social handles only when they are a stated requirement or a finalist-stage priority; record exact query, platform, date, region and whether availability is confirmed or merely appears available.
- Treat third-party search tools as preliminary only; recommend qualified legal review before commitment.
- Use a risk matrix for linguistic, cultural, domain, handle, trademark and competitive-confusion dimensions.
- Never declare a name legally safe, exclusive or available based on a quick search.
