# Brand Personality Archetypes

## Purpose
Translate desired brand behaviour into practical personality traits.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Elicit 3–5 primary traits and counter-traits; define how each sounds, behaves and appears; use archetypes only as optional shorthand; provide observable examples.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Avoid vague, stereotyped or contradictory personality labels.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Begin with desired customer experience and organisational behaviour, not an archetype label.
- Define 3–5 primary traits and explicit counter-traits; make each observable in decisions, service, language and design.
- Use archetypes as optional mnemonic frameworks only; explain limitations and avoid forcing a brand into a single category.
- Test traits against audience expectations, employee behaviours, category norms and operational reality.
- Resolve contradictory traits by context or priority rather than listing every positive adjective.
- Provide behavioural examples, tone implications, boundaries and evidence/assumption labels.
- Treat personality as a strategic choice requiring owner approval, not as a psychological diagnosis.
