# Naming Studio

## Purpose
Generate distinctive, strategically grounded brand and product names.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Read `../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/NAMING_METHOD_PLAYBOOK.md` before naming work. Apply its strategy-first brief, divergent ideation territories, verbal tests, scorecard and staged presentation principles. Set the naming role, language, markets, audience, tone and hard constraints; explore descriptive, suggestive, metaphorical, evocative, real-word, unexpected-word, coined, constructed and compound routes. Do not default to obvious keyword blends or force a clever etymology. Produce a broad raw batch with exact spelling and a concise one-line territory/construction note only. Hand the batch immediately to `brand-name-availability` before writing detailed rationales, stories, taglines or identity territories. After the viability gate, develop only candidates marked PASS or explicitly accepted for review.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Treat every name as unvalidated until screening is completed.
- Do not spend creative time producing detailed concepts for candidates before the mandatory availability gate.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Confirm naming role (corporate, product, service, feature, campaign), desired meaning, audience, markets, languages and hard constraints.
- Read and follow the shared Naming Method Playbook; explore genuinely different territories rather than producing variations of one formula.
- Evaluate candidates as spoken and remembered objects: first-sight pronunciation, hearability, spelling from dictation, delayed recall, associations, visual form and cross-language risks.
- Use the default early scorecard from the playbook unless the brief justifies different weights; keep creative scoring separate from mandatory legal/domain gates.
- Avoid over-engineered portmanteaux and names whose rationale is needed to explain or rescue the word.
- Generate a broad raw batch; do not over-invest in early shortlisting or concept development.
- Run `brand-name-availability` immediately after generation. Mandatory domain failures (including .pt/.com when required) are hard stops unless the owner changes the constraint.
- Develop the strategic shortlist only from candidates that pass or are explicitly accepted for review; then assess pronounceability, spelling, recall, relevance, distinctiveness and extensibility.
- Explain name construction and intended associations; flag accidental meanings and pronunciation risks as hypotheses for native review.
- Avoid names that imply unsupported capabilities, regulated status, guaranteed outcomes or misleading affiliation.
- Keep ideation separate from validation; do not describe candidates as available until formal checks are completed.
- Provide concise rationale and a handoff brief for `brand-name-availability`; after that gate, hand passing candidates to `brand-naming-validation` for linguistic, cultural and confusion-risk analysis.
