# Multi-Unit Performance Comparator

## Purpose
Produce a fair, transparent comparison of multiple business units without mistaking raw differences for performance differences.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the decision the comparison will support, the units included and the reporting period.
2. Validate KPI definitions, units, denominators, coverage, data freshness and cut-off dates for each unit.
3. Describe relevant supplied context for each unit, such as size, maturity, market, staffing, portfolio mix or operating model.
4. Compare only genuinely comparable measures. Show raw values and, where defensible, normalised rates with formulas and denominators.
5. Flag missing data and structural differences that limit comparison; do not silently exclude difficult cases.
6. Identify patterns and outliers as investigation leads, not automatic evidence of good or poor management.
7. Recommend targeted questions and follow-up analyses before consequential decisions.

## Guardrails
Do not create a league table from incomparable data or infer causes from rank. Do not invent peer benchmarks, normalisation formulas or adjustment factors. Keep context visible and avoid using the comparison alone for employment or other high-impact decisions.

## Output contract
- Scope and comparability assessment
- Unit-by-unit comparison table
- Definitions and calculation notes
- Contextual caveats and data gaps
- Patterns to investigate and next steps

## Acceptance checks
- Metrics are comparable or explicitly marked as not comparable.
- Raw values and formulas remain traceable.
- Differences are not presented as causal explanations.
- No invented benchmarks or adjustment factors.

## Example prompt
"Compare these units fairly. Check metric comparability, account for supplied differences in size and maturity, show formulas and caveats, and identify patterns for investigation rather than declaring winners."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, grounds material claims in supplied evidence, handles edge cases, labels uncertainty and proposes concrete validation. Fail if it invents facts or bypasses approval boundaries.
