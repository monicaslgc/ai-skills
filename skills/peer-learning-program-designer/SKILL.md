# Peer Learning Program Designer

## Purpose
Turn informal knowledge sharing into a repeatable programme with practical outputs and evidence of adoption.

## Procedure
1. Clarify the learning objective, audience, recurring challenge and intended operational outcome.
2. Identify candidate practices or case examples and request evidence that they worked in their original context.
3. Design a session format with preparation, a focused case walkthrough, questions, constraints and reusable takeaways.
4. Include context needed to judge transferability, including team size, tools, process and customer differences.
5. Agree an experiment or follow-up action only where a receiving team chooses to test the practice.
6. Define measures of usefulness and adoption, plus a feedback loop for improving later sessions.
7. Document approved reusable artefacts and assign ownership for follow-up.

## Guardrails
Do not present anecdotes as proven best practice. Obtain permission before sharing sensitive cases or personal data. Do not assume a practice transfers unchanged across teams. Keep participation and adoption claims evidence-based.

## Output contract
- Programme objective and audience
- Session agenda and facilitation guide
- Practice case template
- Transferability questions and optional pilot
- Feedback, adoption and follow-up measures

## Acceptance checks
- Practices include context and supporting evidence.
- Receiving teams can adapt or reject a practice.
- Confidential details are excluded or approved for sharing.
- Learning and adoption are measured separately.

## Example prompt
"Design a peer-learning session for these teams. Create an agenda, a reusable case template, transferability questions and a practical follow-up method without treating anecdotes as proof."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, grounds material claims in supplied evidence, handles edge cases, labels uncertainty and proposes concrete validation. Fail if it invents facts or bypasses approval boundaries.
