# Divergent Ideation

## Purpose
Open up the solution space. Produce a range of meaningfully different possibilities before evaluating them.

## Inputs
Use the user's challenge, desired outcome, context and constraints. If key context is missing, either ask one material question or state a reasonable working assumption and proceed.

## Procedure
1. **Set the divergence goal.** Identify what needs to be explored: breadth, novelty, alternative frames, mechanisms, experiences, narratives or combinations.
2. **Choose a generation approach.** Select one or two methods that fit:
   - SCAMPER for transforming an existing idea or experience
   - What if...? for speculative possibilities
   - Reverse brainstorming for exploring the opposite or failure state
   - Forced connections and analogies for cross-domain leaps
   - First principles for questioning inherited assumptions
   - Morphological combinations for mixing solution dimensions
   - Crazy 8s for rapid sketch-like directions (describe in text if no drawing tool exists)
   - Worst possible idea for unlocking a stuck session
3. **Generate distinct directions.** Vary the underlying mechanism, audience, interaction, business model, medium, emotional experience or framing—not just names and wording.
4. **Push beyond the obvious.** Include safe, familiar directions and unexpected or experimental ones when appropriate.
5. **Add a short rationale.** Explain the central move or why the direction is different. Do not evaluate feasibility unless requested.
6. **Invite a next move.** Ask which direction to bounce, combine, mutate or explore further. If the user asked for a large batch, provide it without forcing a question.

## Quality rules
- Prefer diversity over arbitrary quantity.
- Do not produce near-duplicates to fill a list.
- Avoid defaulting to apps, dashboards, AI, gamification or automation unless the challenge supports them.
- Use concrete, vivid descriptions rather than vague labels.
- Keep ideas at comparable levels of abstraction.
- Preserve unusual ideas; mark them as speculative rather than dismissing them.
- Do not claim originality in the world or market without research.
- Do not add unsupported claims about users, trends, costs or technical feasibility.

## Optional idea format
For each direction: **Working name**, **Core move** (what changes?), **How it could work** (a concise illustration), **Why it is different** (the distinct mechanism or frame), and **Open question** (what needs exploration?). Use a lighter format for conversational sessions.

## Acceptance checks
- Ideas address the stated challenge.
- Directions differ in mechanism or framing, not only wording.
- The set includes more than obvious first-order solutions when appropriate.
- Evaluation is deferred unless explicitly requested.
- Assumptions and speculative elements are labelled.
- The user has a clear way to continue exploring.

## Example prompt
"Generate eight genuinely different ways to help neighbours exchange skills. Include conventional, unexpected and speculative directions. Do not rank them."
