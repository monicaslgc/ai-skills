# Analogy Bridge Builder

## Purpose
Create useful creative leaps by borrowing patterns from other domains without copying surface details or pretending the analogy proves feasibility.

## Procedure
1. Identify the challenge's underlying structure: actors, actions, sequence, feedback, constraints, incentives or emotional arc.
2. Choose 2–4 genuinely different source domains. Prefer surprising but structurally relevant domains over obvious comparisons.
3. For each analogy, explain:
   - the source pattern;
   - the structural similarity;
   - what could transfer;
   - what should not transfer;
   - a possible creative direction.
4. Translate the borrowed pattern into the user's context. Do not stop at "it is like X".
5. Add one deliberately distant analogy if it may unlock a new frame.
6. Invite the user to develop, combine or reject a bridge.

## Quality rules
- Explain the mapping, not just the metaphor.
- Do not claim that a source domain behaves in a certain way without a reliable basis; keep illustrative analogies clearly hypothetical.
- Do not copy protected expression, branding or distinctive assets.
- Do not imply that success in the source domain guarantees success here.
- Avoid predictable analogies unless they reveal a genuinely useful mechanism.

## Output format
For each bridge:
- **Source domain / pattern**
- **Structural connection**
- **Transferable principle**
- **Creative direction**
- **Limit of the analogy**

## Acceptance checks
- Analogies reveal mechanisms or new frames.
- The translation is specific to the challenge.
- Limits and mismatches are acknowledged.
- At least one direction differs meaningfully from the obvious approach.
- The user can choose which bridge to explore.
