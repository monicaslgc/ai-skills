# Daily Operations Review

## Purpose
Prepare a concise daily review of operational health, exceptions, decisions and next actions.

## Procedure
1. Confirm the operation, review period, participants, service commitments and decisions the meeting can make.
2. Collect only the supplied evidence: volume, backlog, ageing, service levels, incidents, staffing/availability and prior actions. Record source and timestamp.
3. Compare actuals with an agreed baseline or target. If none exists, report the observed value without inventing a target.
4. Separate normal variance, emerging risk, active incident, decision needed and information-only item.
5. Prioritise by customer/business impact, urgency, duration and reversibility; explain the ordering.
6. Create an action log with one accountable owner, due date only when supplied or agreed, dependency and success condition.
7. Close with decisions, unresolved questions and the next review trigger.

## Guardrails
Do not turn the meeting into a status dump. Do not infer blame or invent owners, targets, root causes or deadlines. Escalate safety, legal, security, financial-control or customer-impact concerns through the user's established process. Keep personal and customer data minimised.

## Output contract
- Executive summary (3–5 bullets)
- Operational indicators and evidence timestamps
- Exceptions ranked by impact and urgency
- Decisions made / decisions required
- Action log: action, owner, due date, dependency, success condition
- Open questions and next review trigger

## Acceptance checks
- Every material statement has a source or is labelled as an assumption.
- Targets are only used when provided.
- Actions are specific and ownership is not invented.
- High-impact exceptions are visible and not buried in routine updates.

## Example prompt
"Prepare a concise daily review of operational health, exceptions, decisions and next actions. Use only supplied or approved data, label assumptions and unknowns, and include a validation plan."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, labels assumptions, covers at least one exception or boundary case, avoids unsupported claims, and includes a concrete validation or follow-up measure.
