# Competitive Brand Research

## Purpose
Compare competitor brands and adjacent-category references.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Define direct competitors, substitutes and adjacent inspiration categories; collect positioning, audience, offer, tone, visual cues and proof from cited sources; map patterns and whitespace.

## Output
1. **Outcome:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Evidence:** source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts.
4. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required, if any.
5. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not confuse inspiration with competitive evidence or infer private strategy.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Agree category, geography, customer segment and comparison purpose before selecting competitors.
- Distinguish direct competitors, substitutes, aspirational references and adjacent-category examples.
- Compare like with like: offer, audience, price/positioning where public, claims, proof, channels and experience.
- Cite primary sources with access dates and preserve screenshots/notes only where permitted.
- Separate observed public expression from inference about internal strategy or performance.
- Identify category conventions, overused codes and possible whitespace; do not equate unusual with desirable or defensible.
- State sample limitations and avoid claiming an exhaustive competitor set without a defined search method.
