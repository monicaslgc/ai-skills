# Brand Launch Planner

## Purpose
Plan an internal and external brand launch.

## Required inputs
- User objective and required decision or deliverable.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Existing source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
Map audiences, stakeholders, assets, channels, dependencies, training, approvals, rollout waves, risks and measurement; define launch gates.

## Output
1. Outcome: requested artefact or recommendation.
2. Evidence: source, date and confidence for material factual claims.
3. Assumptions and unknowns, separate from verified facts.
4. Decisions needed, owner and approval required.
5. Smallest useful next step.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates.
- Protect confidential and personal information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not announce dates or commitments that have not been approved.
- Follow the shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Separate internal readiness from external launch readiness; define launch audiences, markets, channels and release waves.
- Build a dependency plan covering approved strategy, assets, website/product changes, legal review, staff enablement, support readiness and localisation.
- Define go/no-go criteria, accountable owner, rollback/contingency plan and escalation path.
- Include internal communications and training before asking teams to represent the new brand externally.
- Map every external deliverable to an approved version and named approver.
- Define baseline and post-launch measures with collection owner and review dates.
- Treat dates, budgets and commitments as provisional until authorised; never publish or schedule externally without explicit approval.
