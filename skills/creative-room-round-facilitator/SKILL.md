# Creative Room Round Facilitator

## Purpose
Run one purposeful round of a simulated Creative Room. This skill is model-invoked by a session orchestrator or used when a user explicitly requests a perspective round.

## Before the round
Identify:
- the current challenge or idea;
- what the round should unlock (breadth, depth, reframing, critique or connection);
- what has already been explored;
- constraints that must remain intact.

If the goal is unclear but the challenge is usable, choose a sensible round objective and state it briefly.

## Facilitation sequence
1. **Set the question.** Frame one productive question for this round.
2. **Select lenses.** Choose 2–4 perspectives; do not use all by default.
3. **Contribute independently.** Give each lens a distinct move before allowing synthesis.
4. **Create interaction.** Let one lens build on, challenge or reinterpret another contribution. Avoid scripted debate.
5. **Surface a useful tension.** Identify a real trade-off, incompatible premise or unanswered question when one exists.
6. **Invite the user in.** Ask for a reaction or offer one concrete next move.
7. **Record carry-forward material.** Preserve promising ideas, tensions and unresolved questions for the next round.

## Round types
- **Open:** expand the possibility space.
- **Reframe:** question how the challenge is defined.
- **Deepen:** explore the mechanism or experience behind one direction.
- **Challenge:** test assumptions and unintended consequences.
- **Connect:** combine patterns or ideas from different directions.
- **Reset:** change the method when the room is repeating itself.

## Guardrails
- This is a facilitation method, not a multi-agent runtime.
- Do not fabricate disagreement for drama.
- Do not force a vote, ranking or consensus.
- Do not make every lens speak every round.
- Keep the user as participant and decision-maker.
- Separate a strong creative proposition from a validated or feasible solution.

## Output contract
A useful round contains:
- Round objective
- Selected lenses and distinct contributions
- Interaction or productive tension
- Carry-forward ideas / open questions
- One invitation for the user

## Acceptance checks
- The round has a clear objective.
- Perspectives are meaningfully different.
- Interaction creates value rather than theatre.
- The session remains open to user correction.
- The round ends with a useful continuation point.
