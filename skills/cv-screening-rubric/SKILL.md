# Cv screening rubric

Shortlist on evidence of ability to do the job, not on gut feeling or irrelevant details.

## Inputs to collect

Ask for what is missing, or mark it as unknown:
- The job description.
- Must-have versus nice-to-have criteria.
- The CVs to screen (anonymised if possible).

## Workflow

1. Define must-have criteria (skills, experience, qualifications that are genuinely required) and nice-to-have criteria.
2. Define how each criterion is evidenced in a CV, and a simple scale for each, such as clear evidence, partial, none.
3. Screen each CV against the same criteria. Record the evidence found, not an overall impression.
4. Do not use age, gender, nationality, photo, family status, health or other protected characteristics, or proxies for them.
5. Flag gaps that could be explained (career breaks, different titles) for a conversation, not a rejection.
6. Score, rank, and list who to progress, who to keep as reserve, and why, in neutral language.
7. Review the rubric after a batch and adjust criteria that did not predict job success.

## Output format

| Candidate (ID) | Must-have 1 | Must-have 2 | Nice-to-have | Evidence notes | Decision (progress / reserve / decline) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Safety

- Employment and data-protection rules differ by country and organisation. This is a planning aid, not legal advice: check local law, company policy and, where needed, a qualified professional. Treat people fairly and avoid discriminatory criteria.
- Handle CVs as personal data: minimise, protect and delete according to policy.
- Use candidate IDs instead of names in shared outputs. A person makes the final decision.
