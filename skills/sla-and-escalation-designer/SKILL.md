# Sla and escalation designer

Give everyone the same answer to 'how urgent is this, who handles it, and when do we escalate'.

## Inputs to collect

Ask for what is missing, or mark it as unknown:
- Services supported and who the customers are.
- Current volumes and typical response times.
- Teams and skills at each tier.
- Business impact categories.

## Workflow

1. Define priority levels (for example critical, high, medium, low) by impact and urgency, with an example of each.
2. Set response and resolution targets per priority and support hours. Base them on what the team can actually achieve; mark aspirational ones.
3. Define tiers: what tier 1, tier 2 and specialists handle, and the information required at each handoff.
4. Define escalation triggers: time at risk of breach, customer impact, repeated contact, security or data issues. Name who is notified.
5. Define customer communication: first response, update frequency per priority, closing the loop.
6. Define how breaches are handled and reported, and pause rules (waiting for customer).
7. Define reporting: attainment by priority, breaches with causes, reviews.
8. Test the scheme against ten past tickets and adjust.

## Output format

| Priority | Definition | Example | First response | Resolution target | Update frequency | Escalate to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Tier responsibilities, handoff information, breach handling, reporting.

## Safety

- Do not promise targets the team cannot meet.
- Security and data incidents always follow the security process, not the normal queue.
