# Operational Controls Checklist Builder

## Purpose
Translate a repeatable operational task into a checklist that prevents omissions and makes exceptions auditable.

## Procedure
1. Identify the process, failure modes, control objective, user roles and point in the workflow where the check is needed.
2. Separate mandatory policy or contractual controls from proposed good practice; ask for source material when requirements are unknown.
3. For each check, define the action/question, expected evidence, pass/fail/ not-applicable conditions and what constitutes an exception.
4. Assign frequency or trigger, responsible role, reviewer and record location only where supplied or agreed.
5. Order checks to match the real workflow and avoid duplicate controls or checks that cannot be evidenced.
6. Define escalation, remediation, recheck and evidence-retention requirements from approved policy.
7. Test the checklist against a normal case, a missing-input case and a failure/exception case; revise ambiguous items.

## Guardrails
Never invent legal or regulatory requirements or claim compliance from checklist completion alone. Keep control bypasses explicit and authorised. Avoid collecting unnecessary personal data or secrets in evidence records.

## Output contract
- Purpose, scope and source policies
- Checklist table: step, control objective, evidence, pass/fail rule
- Frequency/trigger and responsible role
- Exception and escalation path
- Evidence and retention notes
- Test cases and unresolved policy questions

## Acceptance checks
- Each item is observable and can be evidenced.
- Mandatory requirements cite supplied policy or are marked unknown.
- Exceptions have a defined next step.
- Normal and failure cases have been tested.

## Example prompt
"Translate a repeatable operational task into a checklist that prevents omissions and makes exceptions auditable. Use only supplied or approved data, label assumptions and unknowns, and include a validation plan."

## Evaluation
Pass if the output follows the contract, labels assumptions, covers at least one exception or boundary case, avoids unsupported claims, and includes a concrete validation or follow-up measure.
