# Brand Strategy

## Purpose
Turn discovery evidence into a coherent set of strategic choices that can guide product, service, operations, people and communications.

## Knowledge dependencies
- **Mode:** Hybrid.
- **Required knowledge:** [Brand Fundamentals](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/BRAND_FUNDAMENTALS.md), [Strategy and Positioning](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/STRATEGY_AND_POSITIONING.md).
- **Supporting knowledge:** [Research and Evidence](../../knowledge/brand-strategy/RESEARCH_AND_EVIDENCE.md) when available and relevant.
- Read the required knowledge before synthesising strategy. Use the concepts and decision criteria; do not reproduce the knowledge base as generic exposition.
- If knowledge files are inaccessible, state that limitation, use only the principles available in this skill and avoid claiming the full knowledge base was consulted.

## Required inputs
- The user's objective and the decision or deliverable required.
- Relevant organisation, product, audience, market and language context.
- Discovery evidence, source material, constraints and approved decisions.
- Unknowns; ask only for information that materially changes the work.

## Method
1. Define the decision the strategy must support.
2. Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and approved fact.
3. Synthesize purpose/role, ambition, priority audience, customer problem, promise/value, differentiators and reasons to believe.
4. Connect every proposed choice to evidence, organisational capability and experience implications.
5. Identify trade-offs, exclusions, unresolved tensions and evidence that would change the recommendation.
6. Test coherence: audience need ↔ promise ↔ capability ↔ proof ↔ delivered experience.
7. Present options where the evidence does not justify a single conclusion; request owner approval.

## Output
1. **Strategic recommendation:** the requested artefact or recommendation.
2. **Strategic logic:** audience, problem, value, differentiation, proof and category context where relevant.
3. **Evidence:** source, date, context and confidence for material factual claims.
4. **Assumptions and unknowns:** separate from verified facts and approved decisions.
5. **Trade-offs and non-goals:** what the strategy prioritises and deliberately does not do.
6. **Experience implications:** what product, service, operations, people and communications must deliver.
7. **Decisions needed:** owner and approval required.
8. **Next step:** the smallest useful action.

## Quality and safety
- Preserve the user's terminology and supplied source facts; do not silently fill gaps.
- Separate evidence, interpretation, hypothesis and recommendation.
- Prefer primary sources and record URLs and access dates for research.
- Protect confidential, personal and commercially sensitive information; use synthetic examples in public artefacts.
- Do not produce slogans or visual directions as substitutes for strategy.
- Do not treat purpose, mission, vision and values as mandatory boxes; include them only when they clarify strategic choices.
- Do not claim differentiation, leadership, uniqueness or impact without relevant evidence.
- Follow shared contracts in ../../kits/brand-strategy-identity/README.md when tools are available. If a tool is unavailable, state the limitation and provide a manual alternative.

## Decision rules
- Synthesize evidence into strategic choices; do not merely assemble statements.
- Distinguish intended identity from audience image and reputation.
- Define purpose/role, ambition, priority audience, customer problem, promise, value proposition, differentiators and reasons to believe where supported.
- For each choice, state evidence, implication, trade-off, confidence and what the organisation will not do.
- Test whether capabilities and operating model can consistently deliver the promise.
- Provide principles that guide product, service, hiring and communications—not only marketing copy.
- Present options and unresolved tensions for owner approval; only approved choices enter Brand Context as normative.
