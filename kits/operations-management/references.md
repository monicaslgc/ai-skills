# Operations management references

Use these as conceptual references and verify current editions or local requirements before applying them. The skills do not replace company policy, specialist advice or an approved management system.

## Reference areas

- **Process and value-stream thinking:** map work from intake to outcome; distinguish processing time from waiting, handoffs and rework.
- **Operational performance:** define every KPI with purpose, unit, denominator, time window, owner and source; review quality and service alongside volume and speed.
- **Capacity and flow:** compare demand with usable capacity by skill and work type; make assumptions about variability and non-queue work explicit.
- **Incident and exception management:** use an approved severity matrix, clear escalation triggers, containment boundaries, recovery criteria and a learning review.
- **Continuous improvement:** state a falsifiable hypothesis, measure a baseline, define a counter-metric and use a stop/rollback condition.
- **Operational controls:** define the control objective, evidence, frequency, exception route and reviewer; do not infer compliance from a completed checklist alone.
- **Business performance and variance:** verify KPI definitions and comparability before interpreting changes; distinguish arithmetic contributors from causal explanations.
- **Recovery and prioritisation:** use explicit baselines, milestones, decision gates and transparent criteria; protect mandatory obligations from ordinary weighted scoring.
- **Management reviews and follow-through:** report decisions required, accountable actions, dependencies and outcome evidence; distinguish activity completion from realised benefit.
- **Multi-unit assessment:** account for scale, maturity, local context and data quality before comparing units; avoid ranking teams using unadjusted figures.

## Public starting points

- Lean Enterprise Institute — Lean Lexicon and value-stream concepts: https://www.lean.org/
- APQC — Process classification and benchmarking resources: https://www.apqc.org/
- NIST — Risk Management Framework and control concepts, where relevant to the operation: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework
- ITIL — service management concepts and practices: https://www.peoplecert.org/

These links are starting points, not evidence that a particular process complies with a standard. Prefer approved internal SOPs, contracts, service definitions and legal/regulatory guidance for binding requirements.

## Before using a reference

1. Check the source's scope and publication date.
2. Separate mandatory policy from optional practice.
3. Translate concepts into observable checks and evidence.
4. Confirm the responsible process owner approves changes.
