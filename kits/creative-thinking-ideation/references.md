# Creative Thinking & Ideation references

## Working principles

These skills use broadly established creative-thinking practices. They are not tied to one proprietary framework or a claim that one method works for every challenge.

- **Divergent and convergent thinking:** separate the generation of possibilities from their evaluation. Move between them as needed; do not converge simply because a list exists.
- **Yes, and:** acknowledge and extend a contribution before introducing a new direction. This is a useful collaboration stance, not a rule to agree with every claim.
- **SCAMPER:** prompts for Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate and Reverse.
- **Six Thinking Hats:** use distinct modes of attention (information, feelings, caution, benefits, creativity and process) rather than treating them as fixed personalities.
- **Reverse brainstorming:** explore how to cause or worsen the problem, then invert the observations into possible interventions.
- **Forced connections and analogies:** borrow structures or principles from another domain, then test whether the connection is useful rather than merely decorative.
- **First-principles questioning:** separate basic constraints from inherited assumptions and conventions.
- **Morphological analysis:** identify dimensions of a challenge and explore combinations of possible values.
- **What-if prompts:** explore counterfactuals and boundary conditions.
- **Crazy 8s:** rapidly sketch or describe eight directions to encourage breadth; the number is a facilitation prompt, not a quality measure.
- **Worst possible idea:** deliberately generate bad or absurd approaches to expose assumptions and unlock alternatives.

## Facilitation guidance

1. Pick techniques based on the current creative blockage or goal.
2. Explain a technique briefly only when that helps the user participate.
3. Do not use a framework as decoration; every prompt should change the thinking.
4. Treat outputs as hypotheses or possibilities, not evidence.
5. Adapt language, examples and complexity to the user and domain.
6. Respect accessibility, cultural context and constraints supplied by the user.
7. Never claim a concept is original, validated, safe, legal or feasible without appropriate evidence.

## Additional facilitation methods

- **Perspective-taking:** deliberately change the question being asked (possibility, purpose, experience, mechanism, critique or synthesis). A lens is a temporary mode of attention, not a persona.
- **Creative facilitation rounds:** define a purpose for each round, select only useful perspectives, allow interaction, capture carry-forward material and invite participant direction.
- **Tension mapping:** make competing values and assumptions explicit before exploring sequencing, segmentation, adjustable boundaries or experiments. This avoids false compromise.
- **Analogical reasoning:** map structural similarities across domains, translate a pattern into the target context and identify where the analogy breaks.
- **Constraint manipulation:** distinguish fixed constraints from assumptions, then change one variable deliberately to discover new possibilities. Restore hard constraints before assessing directions.

These methods are used as practical prompts, not as claims of validated outcomes or universal recipes.


## Convergence and concept development

- **Affinity and pattern synthesis:** group observations or ideas by underlying meaning, need or mechanism. Clusters are working interpretations, not objective truth; retain outliers and contradictory evidence.
- **Concept synthesis:** combine compatible elements into a coherent proposition while preserving traceability to the source ideas. A combination should add value, not simply collect features.
- **Concept framing:** articulate the intended change, audience or context, core proposition and high-level experience before moving into detailed requirements.
- **Design principles:** express a small set of qualities that should guide future choices and protect the concept's intent.
- **Assumption mapping:** separate what is known, assumed, constrained and still unknown. A concept brief is not evidence of validation.
- **Iterative convergence:** move between pattern finding, combination, divergence and development. A useful synthesis may reveal a new question that requires another exploratory round.

These practices are used to support creative judgement. They do not replace research, feasibility analysis, prioritisation or user approval.
